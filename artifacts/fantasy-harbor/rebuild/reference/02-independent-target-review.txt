ART CRITIQUE
The target depicts an attractive, believable adult traveler in tailored layered clothing, and a richly occupied Mediterranean fantasy bay. Its appeal comes first from adult anatomy, a relaxed narrow silhouette, and an enclosing sweep of buildings, shore, cliffs and ruins. Warm sun, cool turquoise water and physically varied surfaces support that structure.
The prior runtime shows a toy-like child figure with an oversized round head, very short limbs and simplified clothing. Round clustered tree crowns, broad empty ground and a single isolated building produce a sparse park rather than an inhabited coastal destination. The lighthouse is present, but does not establish the target's cliff-backed bay. Flat olive/beige color fields and limited depth separation further flatten the scene. These are structural mismatches; surface decoration alone cannot resolve them.
The runtime camera is not comparable to the target. It cannot prove the full bay is absent elsewhere, but the submitted image does not demonstrate it. Movement evidence proves movement only.

FIXED ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA — GATED IN THIS ORDER
1. SILHOUETTE / PROPORTION / COMPOSITION
Adult traveler: approximately 7–7.5 heads tall, with plausible shoulder/hip relationship, long articulated limbs, recognizable hands and boots, a shaped face, and hair constructed as an intentional hairstyle. Layered jacket, shirt, trousers, belt and satchel must read as clothing with thickness, seams and fitted transitions. No oversized baby head, peg limbs, spherical mitten hands or exposed primitive assembly. The character must read as the same adult from front, side, rear and a normal gameplay distance. Exact pore detail is not required for this gate.
Bay: a broad curved shoreline and navigable pier anchor the foreground; village buildings and terraces occupy one shore; a large spreading tree and ruined stone arches establish the middle distance; elevated cliffs and a lighthouse close the distant view. Sea remains a substantial readable blue/turquoise area. Architecture, coast and vegetation must overlap in distinct foreground/middle/background layers, with a clear visual route into the town. Preserve the concept's intimate human scale and inhabited density; an open lawn populated by isolated props does not pass.
Evidence: full-body front/side/rear character renders, one face/hands close-up, and an actual scene/runtime capture approximating the concept's scene panel camera. An overhead navigation capture may supplement, but cannot replace, the comparable scene view.
2. LIGHTING
After gate 1 passes: warm directional sunlight, cooler open-sky fill, grounded contact shadows and clear distance separation. The traveler must remain readable against the scene. Water must show coherent reflection and shallow/deep variation; cliffs and buildings must retain form rather than becoming uniform flat fields. Lanterns may add warmth without replacing daylight structure.
3. MATERIALS
After gates 1–2 pass: visually distinct skin, cloth, leather, hair, aged wood, stone, plaster, terracotta, foliage and water. Variation must follow construction: plank direction, masonry joints, roof tiles, cloth folds, boot and satchel edges. Avoid uniformly glossy plastic, repetitive noise and color-only substitutions for modeled form. Material detail cannot compensate for incorrect body or building proportions.
4. DETAIL
After gates 1–3 pass: architectural openings and roof/eave articulation, terraces/stairs, climbing plants and flowers, pier posts and rope rails, plus a restrained set of barrels/crates/nets/furniture and a boat. Distribute detail in useful clusters around routes, thresholds and working waterfront edges. Break repeated shapes and maintain clear walkable space. Details must reinforce the established place rather than clutter an unfinished layout.

REVIEW RULE
Pass requires each gate to achieve at least 7/10 against the unchanged concept, with no unresolved critical silhouette or proportion defect. A failed lower gate blocks approval of higher gates. Target fidelity and technical/runtime validity are separate judgments.
